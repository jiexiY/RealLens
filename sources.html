The environmental method

EMERGE Textbook 1: Data Analysis · Chapter 3, Lesson 3: Vegetation & Water Indices.

NDVI compares near-infrared and red reflectance: (near-infrared − red) / (near-infrared + red). We use this vegetation signal to describe three parts of a mapped campus study line in plain language.

RealLens uses public Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A data from September 22, 2024. This is a single-scene adaptation, not the lesson’s temporal median. The reflectance is scaled using STAC metadata, masked to SCL classes 4, 5, 6, and 7, and reprojected. The source pixels are 10 m; the classification mask is 20 m. Missing pixels remain missing.

Samples every 10 m along each third of the mapped line produce length-weighted means. No descriptive vegetation label is assigned below 50% valid coverage. The bins (below 0.25, 0.25–0.49, 0.50+) are not validated land-cover classes. Historical vegetation does not establish current shade, pavement condition, or safe travel.

The source scene and processing metadata are included in the downloadable grid.

GLOBE Observer and the geoemerge package are not used. The track permits other public environmental data, including Sentinel. This project uses the named EMERGE curriculum method rather than claiming to use every suggested dataset.

Forecasts and published information

NOAA / National Weather Service

NWS public API: hourly rain probability, temperature, wind, and current alerts. Weather is checked when you explicitly prepare or refresh a briefing. The approximately 2.5 km forecast grid cannot reveal individual puddles, local gusts, or slippery surfaces.

UF Campus Closures

Official UF notices and polygons: checked every 15 seconds while the conditions page has a briefing, is visible, and is online. Short caches, source publication, and network delays apply. A geometric overlap is an advisory, not a verified blocked path.

Gainesville RTS

Published rider alerts: checked every 15 seconds on the visible, online bus page, or on request in the voice page. They are not live vehicle positions or ETAs. Publication dates do not prove a notice still applies. Images and embedded content are not interpreted. No background push notifications.

Mapped paths, not navigation

© OpenStreetMap contributors · ODbL supplies the bundled geometry. A shortest connected study line is used to sample environmental context, not to recommend an accessible route. It may contain steps or closures. Building-to-network connections, entrances, crossings, obstacles, and pavement condition are unverified.

The map retrieval timestamp is included in the attributed extract; it is not a field-check date.

Reproduce the analysis and track requirements

NASA and Environmental Data Track brief. The public question is: how can blind and low-vision campus travelers understand environmental context before a journey?

The repository contains the public data, preparation scripts, independent route-analysis checks, source credits, and setup instructions. Run npm install, npm test, npm run check:journeys, and npm run dev. To regenerate the campus analysis, see tools/prepare-campus-satellite.py and the README for geospatial dependencies. geoemerge is not a dependency.

Bus stop announcements

Official RTS GTFS data, Fall 2026 release: 27 routes, 56 ordered patterns, 972 stop locations. The bundled extract was retrieved September 20, 2026. Its published feed range is August 17, 2026–May 2, 2027; individual service calendars vary. Download the attributed extract.

On the separate stop-announcement page, choose your bus route, direction, boarding and destination stops. Start journey requests your phone’s location; raw coordinates stay in memory on your device. This compares phone proximity to scheduled stops, not live buses, running service or detours. Only announcement text, including stop names, is sent for speech.

Location accuracy must be within 50 metres and fixes no more than 15 seconds old. Near-stop reminders require two fresh fixes. A selected route is never inferred or corrected automatically. GPS errors, detours, closely spaced stops and screen locking can cause missed or incorrect reminders. Confirm with the operator before exiting. Voice off, End journey, hiding or leaving the page stops tracking. This is foreground-only and requires a deliberate restart after interruption.

Privacy and voice

No account or analytics. This browser stores reading and voice on/off preferences, an explicitly saved pair of places and duration, and the RTS filter. The current trip’s places, time, and duration pass between app pages in tab-local session storage; no raw briefing, recording, transcript or GPS history is stored there. The weather request sends a campus-area coordinate, not your departure time. Hosting providers may retain standard request logs.

Page introductions use ElevenLabs speech by default. Turn voice off cancels app audio and remembers your preference; it also ends an active stop journey. Browsers may require a tap on Play voice before audio can start. This resumes the same generated clip without requesting it again. Provider failures use browser voice with a notice. RealLens cannot enable your device’s VoiceOver or other screen reader.

Optional voice input sends a microphone recording of up to 20 seconds through the RealLens server for transcription; reply text and information you choose with Read page or Read details are sent for speech generation. RealLens does not persist recordings or transcripts. ElevenLabs may retain them under its policies; its free plan does not provide zero retention. Browser speech may use a remote provider. Microphone access starts only after Talk and permission, with no background listening.

Device screen readers can read controls independently of app audio. Spoken requests receive spoken replies when voice is on; typed requests stay silent unless you request reading aloud. Stop, Escape, leaving the page, or hiding it stops current app audio. Provider privacy policy.

Independent, noncommercial research prototype, not an official UF, NASA, NOAA, RTS, or EMERGE service. Not validated with blind or low-vision participants or in real bus journeys.
